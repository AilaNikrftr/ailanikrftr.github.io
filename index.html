this portrait is drawn by a live Raft cluster's network traffic. ~2,500 nodes are wired to their neighbours; 7 of them (S1–S7) vote and elect a leader. every heartbeat, the leader's messages ripple across the wires and the replies come back. a client writes the picture into the log one band at a time, and a node only lights its wires once that entry is committed on it.
 
what am I looking at?

purple leader · pink candidate · moving streaks are messages crossing the wires: AppendEntries going out, replies coming back, vote requests between servers. wires glow brighter right after traffic crosses them. pink wires belong to nodes that are out of touch: their copy of the log is behind, so a read from them would be stale.

Raft picks consistency over availability (the C in CAP): when you cut the network, only the side with at least 4 of the 7 servers can keep committing. the other side keeps holding elections it can never win, and its terms climb. with pre-vote off (plain Raft), healing the cut lets those inflated terms knock out the healthy leader, and the whole picture goes pink while a new one is elected. with pre-vote on (what etcd does), a server first asks whether it could win (orange messages), the others refuse because they still hear a leader, and healing is seamless.
